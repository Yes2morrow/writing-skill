# Language calibration

Use this file for bilingual or discipline-sensitive copyediting. Preserve content, paragraph order, and citation placement unless a minimal movement is required for grammaticality.

## Chinese academic prose

- Prefer explicit subjects when omission makes agency ambiguous; retain natural subject omission when the referent is stable.
- Break long modifier chains by promoting the main action to a verb. Do not mechanically shorten every sentence.
- Distinguish `表明`, `显示`, `说明`, `意味着`, and `证明` by evidential strength; when in doubt, preserve the source verb.
- Reduce paired bureaucratic verbs such as `进行研究`, `展开探讨`, `实现提升` when a direct verb carries the same meaning.
- Preserve established translated concepts and author-defined terms. Do not alternate synonyms for the same analytical category.
- Use four-character expressions sparingly when they add precision; remove them when they merely raise the register.

## English academic prose

- Prefer finite verbs and concrete subjects where nominal chains obscure action.
- Use active or passive voice according to information focus and disciplinary convention, not a universal preference.
- Preserve hedges, modal verbs, tense, and article-specific terminology. Do not make claims stronger for fluency.
- Avoid thesaurus substitution. Repetition of a stable technical term is usually better than elegant inconsistency.
- Check noun stacks, dangling modifiers, unclear pronouns, and sentence openings overloaded with scene-setting clauses.

## Architecture and digital-media terms

Do not collapse these distinctions during polishing:

- representation / visualization / simulation;
- space / spatial experience / spatial practice;
- model as physical artifact, computational model, data model, or theoretical abstraction;
- interaction / responsiveness / participation;
- automation / augmentation / generation / optimization;
- digital / computational / algorithmic / virtual.

When the intended sense is unclear, retain the original term and flag it rather than choosing for the author.

## Semantic-drift check

Compare each edited passage against the source for:

1. same actors and actions;
2. same polarity and contrast;
3. same causal or correlational relation;
4. same modal and evidential strength;
5. same scope, quantity, chronology, and exceptions;
6. same technical term identity;
7. no new evaluation, example, or implication.
