# Evidence base

This reference records the research used to design the language rules. It is a selective evidence map, not a corpus bundled with the skill. Source pages, abstracts, and open full text were reviewed on 2026-08-24; no copyrighted article text is redistributed here.

## What the comparison supports

Across large corpora and controlled comparisons, machine-generated prose often differs from human prose in distributions of vocabulary, syntax, discourse marking, repetition, and cross-text variability. The direction of individual features is not stable across every model, prompt, language, or genre. Consequently:

- diagnose clusters and local effects rather than treating one word or construction as proof;
- focus editing on precision, semantic variety, agency, and purposeful rhythm;
- evaluate content-preserving revisions, not detector evasion;
- retain disciplinary conventions and non-native author voice;
- keep automated lexical scores subordinate to blind human judgment.

The literature does **not** establish `不是……而是……` as a universal Chinese AI marker. This skill flags recurrent or logically false binary contrasts because they are formulaic and often distort relations; a single warranted contrast remains acceptable.

## Comparative language and detection research

1. Zanotto, S. E., & Aroyehun, S. (2025). “Linguistic and Embedding-Based Profiling of Texts Generated by Humans and Large Language Models.” *EMNLP 2025*. Eleven LLMs and eight domains; human texts showed greater feature variation and more diverse semantic content in the reported analysis. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-main.1163
2. Zamaraeva, O., Flickinger, D., Bond, F., & Gómez-Rodríguez, C. (2025). “Comparing LLM-generated and human-authored news text using formal syntactic theory.” *ACL 2025*. Demonstrates systematic grammar-type distribution differences in a controlled genre. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.443
3. Herbold, S., et al. (2023). “AI, write an essay for me.” Large-scale human/ChatGPT essay comparison; reports fewer discourse and epistemic markers but more nominalizations in generated essays. https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14276
4. Kobak, D., González-Márquez, R., Horvát, E.-Á., & Lause, J. (2025). “Delving into LLM-assisted writing in biomedical publications through excess vocabulary.” More than 15 million abstracts; reports a post-ChatGPT rise dominated by style words. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12219543/
5. Liang, W., et al. (2024). “Mapping the Increasing Use of LLMs in Scientific Papers.” Corpus-level analysis of 950,965 papers; explicitly frames estimates at population rather than individual-text level. https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01268
6. Russell, J., Karpinska, M., & Iyyer, M. (2025). “People who frequently use ChatGPT for writing tasks are accurate and robust detectors of AI-generated text.” Human explanations relied on lexical cues and higher-order judgments such as originality and clarity. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.267
7. Liang, W., Yuksekgonul, M., Mao, Y., Wu, E., & Zou, J. (2023). “GPT detectors are biased against non-native English writers.” Warns that detector use can penalize constrained linguistic expression and can be bypassed by prompting. https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02819
8. Al Ali, A., Helcl, J., & Libovický, J. (2026). “Different Time, Different Language.” A Czech-language reassessment did not reproduce systematic non-native bias across contemporary detectors, illustrating temporal and language dependence. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.eacl-srw.20
9. El Attar, Y., Dönmez, E., Maurer, M., & Falenska, A. (2026). “A Systematic Analysis of Linguistic Features in AI-Generated Text Detection Across Domains and Models.” Tests 284 interpretable features across 27 models and ten domains; reports strong context dependence, with lexical richness comparatively robust. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04177
10. Terčon, L., & Dobrovoljc, K. (2025). “Linguistic Characteristics of AI-Generated Text: A Survey.” Synthesizes recurring findings and highlights concentration on English and GPT-family models. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05136
11. 闫鹏飞、张军（2025）：《生成式AI与专家学者学术写作语体特征的对比预测研究》。以可比摘要语料分析生成式AI与专家写作的多维语体差异。https://cnki.istiz.org.cn/kcms/detail/detail.aspx?dbcode=CJFQ&dbname=CJFD2025&filename=XAWX202502011
12. 《国内外大语言模型生成中文论文摘要对比研究——以图书情报领域为例》（2024）。比较121个题目的模型摘要，并与中文期刊摘要规范对照，报告长度和句数差异。https://doi.org/10.13266/j.issn.2095-5472.2024.032
13. 《人工智能生成文本的词汇丰富度和句法复杂度研究：对比ChatGPT与本族语大学生的写作语篇》。从词汇与句法指标提供实证比较。https://jfl.shisu.edu.cn/article/id/3b270d92-a004-4817-964a-0715e8d3d4ed

## Academic style and copyediting principles

14. Gopen, G. D., & Swan, J. A. (1990). “The Science of Scientific Writing.” *American Scientist*, 78, 550–558. Reader-expectation account of topic and stress positions; supports information-structure revision rather than connector inflation.
15. Hyland, K. (2008). “Genre and academic writing in the disciplines.” *Language Teaching*. Academic conventions vary by disciplinary community, arguing against a universal prose template. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0261444808005235
16. Nature Masterclasses. “Writing a Research Paper: 2nd Edition.” Frames effective writing as informative, concise, well-structured, and engaging; this skill uses only its language-level implications. https://www.nature.com/masterclasses/writing-research-paper/52046480
17. Nature Genetics (2015). “Cause, correlation, conjecture.” Emphasizes accurate representation of claims and cautions against repetitive claim presentation. https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3271
18. Blackman, E. G. (2017). “Science writing: On what's neither clear nor obvious.” Questions unsupported `clearly` and `obviously`, informing the inflated-certainty checks. https://doi.org/10.1038/550457e

## Limitations

- The Chinese evidence base is smaller than the English one.
- Abstracts and published papers already pass through editorial systems and may include AI assistance; “human reference” is not a pure psychological category.
- Lexical overrepresentation is a population signal, not an individual verdict.
- The bundled benchmark is synthetic and intentionally stresses known failure modes. It must be supplemented with fresh, blinded, author-owned passages before claiming general effectiveness.
