# Prose finish without ornament inflation

Use this module when the user asks for `文辞美化`, polish, elegance, fluency, or a more mature academic voice. Beautification is the final language pass; it must not create evidence, argument, or structure.

## What “better” means

Aim for precise diction, visible relations, controlled cadence, and a register suited to the passage. Do not equate beauty with rare words, parallelism, longer sentences, or higher emotional temperature. The best revision often makes the action more exact and lets the evidence carry emphasis.

## The finish ladder

Apply only the lowest rung needed:

1. **Correct:** repair grammar, collocation, reference, punctuation, and bilingual interference.
2. **Specify:** replace an abstract shell with the exact actor, operation, object, or observed change already present in the source.
3. **Contour:** place established context before new information; give the consequential word or qualification sentence-final weight where natural.
4. **Cadence:** vary clause length and syntax according to information load. Follow a dense analytical sentence with a shorter boundary or consequence when the content supports it.
5. **Texture:** choose concrete spatial or operational verbs (`穿过`, `遮蔽`, `回传`, `叠加`; `traverse`, `occlude`, `relay`, `overlay`) only when they name the source action exactly.
6. **Restraint:** remove one redundant conclusion, decorative adjective, or transition after the paragraph already makes the relation clear.

Stop before a rung would add interpretation.

## Register by passage type

- **Empirical and methods prose:** foreground observable operations, measurements, conditions, and limits. Favor terminological stability over lexical color.
- **Theoretical prose:** preserve conceptual recurrence; improve the verbs that relate concepts rather than replacing the concepts with synonyms.
- **Project analysis and architectural criticism:** concrete spatial, material, sensory, and temporal verbs may enrich the sentence. Avoid atmosphere words that are not grounded in described form or experience.
- **Digital-media analysis:** name the operation—sampling, mapping, rendering, prompting, classifying, updating—rather than attributing vague agency to “technology” or “the platform.”

## Sentence-level checks

- Can the reader identify who or what performs the main action?
- Does each adjective distinguish, measure, or delimit something?
- Does the transition name a relation that sentence order cannot already show?
- Is a repeated technical term stabilizing the argument, or is a repeated sentence frame merely templated?
- Does the final sentence add consequence, scope, or tension rather than paraphrase the paragraph?
- Would changing the verb strengthen causality or certainty? If so, restore the source strength.

## Bilingual cautions

Chinese can sustain long sentences when topic, hierarchy, and punctuation are controlled; do not convert every sentence into short subject–verb units. English scholarly prose often gains clarity from finite verbs and restrained noun stacks, but passive voice remains useful when the procedure or object is the information focus. In either language, vary syntax through genuine differences in information structure, never through random synonym substitution.

## Delivery for substantial polishing

Return the finished text first. Then list at most five recurring language decisions, such as removal of echo conclusions, restoration of concrete agency, stabilization of one term, or retention of a warranted contrast. Do not turn the note into an article-structure review.
